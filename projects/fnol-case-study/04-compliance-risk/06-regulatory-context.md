# Regulatory context: Australian references for Sam

The register reflects Australian expectations for general insurers using AI in claims conversations. It names instruments at a high level, without section numbers. Each row says what the instrument covers and which risks in the [risk register](01-risk-register.md) cite it.

| Key | Instrument or source | What it means for Sam | Risks |
|-----|----------------------|------------------------|-------|
| REF-CODE | General Insurance Code of Practice | The industry code of practice for general insurers, published by the Insurance Council of Australia. It sets standards for claims handling, supporting customers experiencing vulnerability (including family and domestic violence), financial hardship, and complaints. Check the current version of the Code. | R04, R05, R06, R11, R12, R13, R19 |
| REF-CLAIMS | Claims handling and settling as a financial service | From 1 January 2022, handling and settling an insurance claim became a financial service under the Corporations Act 2001, through the Financial Sector Reform (Hayne Royal Commission Response) Act 2020. Insurers need an Australian financial services (AFS) licence that covers it. | R01, R12 |
| REF-EHF | General obligation to act efficiently, honestly and fairly | AFS licensees must do all things necessary to ensure the financial services covered by their licence are provided efficiently, honestly and fairly. This now includes claims handling. | R01, R02, R03, R04, R05, R11, R12, R13, R16, R18, R19 |
| REF-UGF | Insurance Contracts Act 1984: duty of utmost good faith | The duty of utmost good faith applies to how insurers deal with policyholders, including how they handle claims. | R01, R12, R16, R19 |
| REF-ADVICE | General advice vs personal advice (Corporations Act 2001) | The Corporations Act distinguishes general advice from personal advice (advice that considers, or could reasonably be expected to consider, a person's objectives, financial situation or needs). Sam is designed to give factual information about the policy and the claims process, and to give neither kind of advice. | R02 |
| REF-MDC | Misleading or deceptive conduct | Misleading or deceptive conduct in relation to financial services is prohibited, including under the Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001 (ASIC Act) and the Corporations Act 2001. What an AI assistant says on the insurer's behalf is the insurer's conduct. | R01, R03, R09, R10 |
| REF-IDR | ASIC Regulatory Guide 271 Internal dispute resolution (RG 271) | Sets requirements for how financial firms handle complaints. It uses a broad definition of complaint: an expression of dissatisfaction where a response or resolution is explicitly or implicitly expected. A customer does not have to use the word "complaint". | R06, R14 |
| REF-AFCA | Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) | The external dispute resolution scheme customers can go to if a complaint is not resolved internally. | R06 |
| REF-PRIV | Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) | Most relevant APPs: APP 3 (collection of solicited personal information), APP 5 (notification of the collection of personal information), APP 6 (use or disclosure), APP 8 (cross-border disclosure), APP 11 (security of personal information). Health information is "sensitive information" under the Act. | R07, R08, R14, R15 |
| REF-NDB | Notifiable Data Breaches scheme (Privacy Act 1988) | Requires notification of eligible data breaches that are likely to result in serious harm. | R07, R08 |
| REF-POLA | Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 | Amended the Privacy Act, including transparency requirements for some automated decisions that use personal information. Check whether and when they apply. | R08, R16 |
| REF-AIEP | Australia's AI Ethics Principles | Eight voluntary principles published by the Australian Government: human, societal and environmental wellbeing; human-centred values; fairness; privacy protection and security; reliability and safety; transparency and explainability; contestability; accountability. | R04, R05, R09, R16 |
| REF-VAISS | Voluntary AI Safety Standard | Voluntary guardrails published by the Australian Government in 2024, covering areas such as accountability, risk management, testing, human oversight, telling end users when they are dealing with AI, and record keeping. Check whether it has been updated or replaced by later government guidance. | R09, R10, R11, R14, R17 |
| REF-REP798 | ASIC Report 798 Beware the gap: Governance arrangements in the face of AI innovation | ASIC's 2024 review of how financial services and credit licensees use and govern AI. It warned that governance can lag behind AI adoption. | R17 |
| REF-CPS230 | APRA Prudential Standard CPS 230 Operational Risk Management | Applies to APRA-regulated entities, including general insurers. Covers operational risk, business continuity and management of material service providers (relevant to an AI model vendor). | R17 |
| REF-CPS234 | APRA Prudential Standard CPS 234 Information Security | Information security requirements for APRA-regulated entities. | R10 |
| REF-DDA | Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth) | Relevant to making the chat service accessible. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are a widely used technical standard for this (a standard, not law). | R13 |

## What this note does not cover

- State and territory laws, which can apply to some claims conversations.
- The insurer's own policies, procedures, product wording and claims guidelines.
- Any instrument not listed in the table above. If a new one matters, add it to the open questions in the [README](README.md) first.

### P01-A (Chloe Martin, form-first), file `results/simulated/transcripts/P01-A.md`

Brief (`personas.yaml`, P01): "Rear bumper dented, the boot still closes, the car drives fine." It says nothing about tail-lights.

Verbatim turns (md line numbers; checked against `P01-A.json` transcript indexes 13 to 18):

- Line 10, customer (opening answer): "No, nobody's hurt. We're both okay and the car's still driveable."
- Line 29, SAM (turn 7), A's fixed step (5): "You mentioned the car can still be driven. Is it safe to drive? For example, are the lights, boot and tail-lights working, with nothing loose or dragging?"
- Line 30, customer: "Yes, it drives fine. The boot still closes. The rear bumper is dented, but nothing's hanging off or dragging. I haven't checked the tail-lights closely yet, so I'm not sure whether they're working."
- Line 33, SAM (turn 8): "Are you able to check them now? If not, I'll record it as \"unsure\" for now, and the consultant can follow it up with you."
- Line 34, customer: "I'm at the lights right now and can't check them myself. I'll record it as unsure, and I'll get them checked before I drive it at night."
- Line 35, SAM (turn 9): "No problem. I've recorded it as \"unsure\"."
- Line 40, customer: "I'm not driving right now, so I'm safe. Sorry, I shouldn't have said I was at the lights."
- Line 57, Sam's summary: "**Safe to drive:** Unsure. It drives fine, but the tail-lights haven't been checked yet"
- Line 63, customer: "Yes, that's all correct."

Why drift: the persona added a doubt its brief does not contain, and then said "I'm at the lights right now", which contradicts the brief (the crash was that morning and she is not driving). It withdrew that one turn later. The simulated preference reason (`preferences/P01.json`) admits it: "I ended up confusing it by saying I was still at the lights." The trigger was A's safe-to-drive question, which names the tail-lights.

### P10-A (Jordan Lee, form-first), file `results/simulated/transcripts/P10-A.md`

Brief (P10): "the front bumper was cracked and a headlight broken. ... It drives fine." Ground truth `drivable: ["yes"]`.

Verbatim turns (checked against `P10-A.json` transcript indexes 12, 15, 16, 17, 23, 24):

- Line 34, customer: "Yes, that's right. I didn't see who did it and there was no note. The front bumper is cracked and a headlight is broken, but it still drives fine."
- Line 41, SAM (turn 8), A's fixed step (5): "You said it still drives fine. With the broken headlight, do you feel the car is safe to drive, including at night?"
- Line 42, customer: "Not really sure about night driving. The headlight is out on that side, so I'd rather not drive it at night until it's fixed. It's been fine in daylight so far."
- Line 43, SAM (turn 9): "That makes sense. I'll note it's drivable in daylight only, with a headlight out. If you'd like a tow at any point, let me know and I can book one."
- Line 65, Sam's summary: "**Drivable:** In daylight only. Headlight out, so not at night."
- Line 70, customer: "Yes, that's all correct. One thing to add: the damage is the front bumper and the headlight, and I haven't checked the other sides closely yet."

Why metric artefact: a car with a broken headlight is not plainly "safe to drive" at night. The persona's answer is a reasonable reading of its own brief, and Sam's "unsure" (plus the flag `drivable_daylight_only_headlight_out`) is the more careful record. The ground truth allows only "yes".

Contrast, P10-B (story-first), `P10-B.md` line 34, SAM (turn 5): "The front bumper is cracked, a headlight is broken, and the car is safe to drive." Sam took the customer's "It still drives fine." (line 31) without asking about the headlight. It recorded `drivable: "yes"` and scores as a success. Here the facts check rewarded the variant that asked less about safety.

### What this means for the recommendation (checked against `ab_results.json` and `metrics.recommend`)

- If both misses counted as successes, task success would be 10 against 10 and rule 1 would not fire.
- Rule 2 would then fire: `paired_basis` est mean A 1025.62 s, B 812.87 s; (1025.62 - 812.87) / 1025.62 = 0.207, so 21% (at least 15%); CSAT B 4.4 is not below A 4.2 - 0.5. Recommendation: still B, but on **modelled** time.
- Rule 3 is not reached either way. Only 7 of 10 preference files exist (see section 4).
- So B stands under the rule, but the evidence is thin. The write-up must say so.
